Research Focus

I’m a population geneticist working in medical entomology. My research focuses on understanding natural mosquito populations from an evolutionary genetics perspective, particularly patterns of genetic diversity, gene flow, population structure, and local adaptation. I’m interested in how these evolutionary processes shape important traits to disease transmission and vector control, including insecticide resistance, mosquito behavior, and vector competence.

Field Work

Extensive field experience in Brazil and Africa, conducting mosquito collections across diverse ecological and epidemiological settings. Experience includes sampling (Aedes, Anopheles) species at different life stages using a variety of collection methods, including human landing catches, larval surveys, light traps, and ovitraps.

Laboratory Work

Experience in basic and advanced molecular biology, genomics, and experimental entomology, including genomic and transcriptomic library preparation for Illumina sequencing; mosquito rearing, handling, and manipulation; tissue dissections; microinjections, including RNAi and embryonic microinjections; in vitro Plasmodium berghei infections; mosquito mating assays; and insecticide resistance bioassays.

Computational Work

Experience in statistical analysis and population-genomic data analysis using R, Python, and Unix/HPC environments. Application of statistical and computational methods to investigate population structure, genetic diversity, demographic history, gene flow, natural selection, and insecticide resistance. Experience with data processing, statistical modeling, hypothesis testing, and data visualization, with an emphasis on reproducible analytical workflows and communicating complex genomic results clearly and effectively.
